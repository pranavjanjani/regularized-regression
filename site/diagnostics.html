1 · Four ways to choose λ, and why they disagree

Cross-validation resamples. AIC and BIC instead penalize model complexity analytically, using the effective degrees of freedom from §2:

$$ \mathrm{AIC} = n\log\frac{\mathrm{RSS}}{n} + 2\,\mathrm{df}(\lambda), \qquad \mathrm{BIC} = n\log\frac{\mathrm{RSS}}{n} + \log(n)\,\mathrm{df}(\lambda) $$

Because $\log n > 2$ for any $n > 7$, BIC charges more per degree of freedom and will always choose at least as much regularization as AIC. Held-out error is shown too — greyed, because selecting on it would spend the test set.

All four curves are min–max rescaled onto [0, 1] so they fit one axis; the dashed verticals mark each criterion's own minimum. Absolute values are in the table.

2 · Effective degrees of freedom

An unpenalized model with nine features uses nine degrees of freedom. A penalized one uses fewer — but not a whole number. For ridge, writing $X = UDV^\top$,

$$ \mathrm{df}(\lambda) = \operatorname{tr}\!\big(X(X^\top X + \lambda I)^{-1}X^\top\big) = \sum_{j} \frac{d_j^2}{d_j^2 + \lambda}, $$

which is the sum of the shrinkage factors from Theory §4.1. For Lasso, the count of non-zero coefficients is an unbiased estimate of df (Zou, Hastie & Tibshirani, 2007), so df is an integer there and drops in steps.

6 · Did the solver actually converge?

Ridge has a closed form, so there is nothing to check. Lasso and Elastic Net are iterative, and a tolerance set too loosely will silently hand you a coefficient that should have been zero.

Objective per sweep

Coordinate descent is a descent method: this can never rise. If yours does, the residual update is wrong.

Largest coefficient change per sweep

This is what the stopping rule watches, plotted as log₁₀. Coordinate descent converges geometrically, so a straight line down is the expected shape; a plateau means the tolerance has been reached.

How to use this page in a write-up

Task 2 asks you to choose λ and justify it. Quoting one CV minimum is the weakest defensible answer. Quoting CV, AIC and BIC, noting that they disagree, showing that the held-out error barely moves across the whole region they disagree over, and then choosing the simplest model in that region — that is the answer the rubric is looking for.
