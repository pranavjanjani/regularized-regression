From Likelihood to Lasso

One idea runs through the whole course: every regularizer is a prior in disguise. Least squares is maximum likelihood; Ridge is MAP with a Gaussian prior; Lasso is MAP with a Laplace prior; Elastic Net is both. We derive each, then build them from scratch inside a surge-pricing engine for Mumbai.

Eight modules

Each module is one lecture plus one lab. The mathematics is developed on the left, the production system is built on the right, and they meet in the middle.

MODULE 01

The linear model & least squares

Design matrices, the normal equations, projection geometry, and what goes wrong when $X^\top X$ is close to singular.
MODULE 02

Maximum likelihood

Gaussian noise gives least squares directly from the likelihood. Estimating $\sigma^2$, and what MLE guarantees asymptotically.
MODULE 03

Bayes & the MAP estimator

Posterior ∝ likelihood × prior. Taking logs turns a prior into a penalty — the result everything else depends on. Built step by step in Walkthrough 2.

MODULE 04

Ridge — the Gaussian prior

Closed form, shrinkage in the SVD basis, effective degrees of freedom, and why correlated features share weight.
MODULE 05

Lasso — the Laplace prior

Soft-thresholding derived by hand, the diamond-corner argument, coordinate descent, and the caveats about selection.
MODULE 06

Elastic Net

The grouping effect, Zou & Hastie's double-shrinkage correction, and when the extra hyperparameter is worth it.
MODULE 07

Beyond the point estimate

The full Gaussian posterior, credible intervals, predictive uncertainty, and empirical Bayes for λ.
MODULE 08

Case study: surge pricing

Demand and supply forecasts, additive pricing, the price cap, and which estimator to deploy.
WALKTHROUGHS

Watch the algorithms run

Step through the normal equations, the MAP construction, ridge shrinkage, Lasso coordinate descent and Bayesian updating, one line of arithmetic at a time.
SEMINAR

Where this ships

Recommenders, genomics, credit scoring, ad ranking and sensor fusion, each mapped to the estimator it relies on.

Which estimator, and when

A starting rule of thumb. The rest of the course is about when to depart from it.

SituationEstimatorPrior on $w$PenaltyWhat it buys you
$n \gg p$, features roughly independent MLE / OLS none (flat)none Unbiased and efficient, with nothing to tune.
Features correlated, all plausibly matter Ridge $\mathcal{N}(0,\tau^2)$$\lambda\lVert w\rVert_2^2$ Stability: correlated columns split the weight rather than competing for it.
$p \gg n$; you need 20 features out of 500 for a compliance review Lasso $\text{Laplace}(0,b)$$\lambda\lVert w\rVert_1$ Exact zeros, giving a model that fits on one page.
Correlated groups, and you still need sparsity Elastic Net Gaussian × Laplace$\lambda_1\lVert w\rVert_1+\lambda_2\lVert w\rVert_2^2$ Drops irrelevant groups, keeps relevant ones intact.
You must quote an uncertainty, not just a number Full posterior $\mathcal{N}(0,\tau^2)$— "₹50 ± ₹10" rather than "₹50".

How to use this site

Theory has the derivations. Algorithms runs them one step at a time, drawing each matrix entry, soft-threshold and coordinate update as it happens; read a derivation, then watch it execute. Lab applies the estimators to the full dataset. Data holds the correlation matrix that explains most of the results.
