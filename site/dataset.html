The Mumbai surge dataset

200 synthetic trips across ten Mumbai pickup zones, built so the correlations you need to handle are actually present. Read this before fitting anything: most surprising results in the lab are explained by the correlation matrix in §3.

2 · The data generating process

Synthetic data, so the truth is known and an estimator can be checked against it. Each trip's surge is drawn as

$$ \text{surge} = 40\,\texttt{is\_peak} + 30\,\texttt{is\_rain} + 20\,\texttt{is\_event} + 25\,\texttt{is\_airport} + \frac{50}{S+1} + 2\,(60 - \texttt{traffic}) + \varepsilon, \quad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 10^2), $$

then clipped to $[0, 150]$. Three consequences carry into every task.

Two terms are non-linear

$50/(S+1)$ and the traffic term are not linear in the raw feature, so a linear model cannot be exactly right. The best achievable RMSE is bounded below by more than the noise σ = 10.

One feature is pure noise

is_weekend has a true coefficient of exactly zero, so any non-zero estimate is fitted noise. That makes it a good probe for whether the penalty is working.

The target is censored

— trips sit exactly at the ₹150 cap. Their true surge was higher and never recorded, so coefficients on the drivers of extreme surge are biased toward zero.

3 · The correlation matrix

Blue is positive, red negative; hover a cell for the exact value. Most of what Ridge, Lasso and Elastic Net do differently on this dataset traces back to this figure.

What to look for

traffic_speed correlates with almost everything, since peak and rain cause it and it in turn drives surge, which makes it the feature most likely to absorb its neighbours' coefficients. historical_demand and open_requests are near-duplicates, a good pair for the "Lasso picks one arbitrarily" demonstration. is_weekend correlates with nothing, as a null feature should.
